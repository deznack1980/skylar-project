In Her Memory

Skylar

In 2017, founder John Archer lost his daughter Skylar to a fentanyl-heroin overdose. She was someone's everything — a daughter, a person full of life, someone who deserved a chance at a different outcome.

Fentanyl-laced heroin took her life before help could reach her. It is a story shared by too many families across Arizona and across America — a crisis that does not stop at any zip code, income level, or family.

Rather than let grief remain private and paralyzing, John Archer made a decision: Skylar's name would stand for hope. Her loss would become the fuel for action. The organization carries her name as a commitment — to fight back with compassion, community, and real, tangible help.

The Founder

John Archer

John Archer is the founder of Skylar's Hope For Change, a Phoenix, Arizona resident, and a father who turned the most devastating loss of his life into a mission of action and compassion.

After losing Skylar in 2017, John began learning firsthand what it meant to face addiction, grief, and an overwhelmed system — and what was missing in the gaps between institutions, clinics, and communities.

He launched Skylar's Hope For Change to be what was missing: mobile, personal, direct. An organization that goes to people rather than waiting for them to arrive. That pairs kindness with action. That refuses to accept that some people are simply beyond help.

His belief: every person, no matter how deep in addiction, homelessness, or trauma, deserves at least one more real chance.

How We Got Here

From Loss to Mission

2017

Skylar Passes Away

John Archer loses his daughter Skylar to a fentanyl-heroin overdose. A family's worst nightmare becomes the seed of a new mission.

2018–

Turning Grief Into Action

John begins learning the landscape of addiction recovery, homelessness, mobile outreach, and the gaps that exist between formal systems and real people in crisis.

Now

Skylar's Hope For Change Launches

The organization forms around six core programs — mobile dental and medical outreach, addiction recovery support, transitional housing, veteran support, homeless outreach, and community partnerships.

Building What's Next

With community support, mobile buses launch, partnerships grow, housing programs open, and more lives get a second chance. This is where you come in.
